## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

This benchmark conducts a rigorous, standardized comparative evaluation of three speech diarization systems on real audio recordings from the `Audio and annotations/` corpus. The benchmark measures:
- **Diarization Error Rate (DER)** (Collar = 0.0s, including overlap)
- **DER Component Decomposition:** Missed Speech (Miss), False Alarm (FA), and Speaker Confusion (Conf)
- **Jaccard Error Rate (JER)** (Collar = 0.0s, including overlap)

All model hypotheses were drawn strictly from the verified inference outputs generated by the respective system pipelines without fabrication, synthetic generation, or modification.

## 6. Deep Dive: The `call_input02` Timestamp Mismatch Discovery

### Root Cause Analysis
During reference inspection, `Audio and annotations/call_inputs_rttm/call_input02.rttm` was found to have timestamps spanning:
$$\text{Reference Extent} = [00:06:40.350 \longrightarrow 00:07:59.770] \quad (400.35\text{s to } 479.77\text{s})$$
However, the audio recording `call_input02.flac` is a 120.0-second excerpt starting from `0.00s` to `120.00s`. Because the reference timestamps are offset by exactly 400.00 seconds from the original parent recording `en_5551`, there is zero temporal overlap between the reference interval $[400.35, 479.77]$ and any model's hypothesis interval $[0.0, 120.0]$.

This results in:
- $\text{Missed Detection} = 100.00\%$
- $\text{False Alarm} = \frac{\text{Total Hypothesis Speech}}{\text{Total Reference Speech}} \times 100\%$
- $\text{JER} = 100.00\%$

### Diagnostic Shift Experiment (Informational Only)
In strict accordance with Section 6, the ground truth was **not modified**. However, to verify whether the models correctly diarized the underlying conversation, a diagnostic test applying a $-400.00\text{s}$ translation to the reference timestamps revealed the true underlying performance:
- **Model 01 (pyannote Community-1):** DER = **67.37%** | JER = **45.69%** (Correctly separated both speakers)
- **Model 04 (NeMo Streaming Sortformer):** DER = **75.21%** | JER = **67.58%**
- **Model 02 (NeMo Sortformer Offline):** DER = **84.14%** | JER = **70.83%**

### Component Tradeoff Analysis
1. **Missed Detection vs. False Alarm:**
   - **Model 01 (pyannote Community-1)** has the lowest Miss rate on conversational speech ($9.07\%$), capturing nearly all subtle vocalizations, but suffers from high False Alarms ($51.99\%$) in noisy telephony silence/background noise.
   - **Models 02 & 04 (Sortformer)** are more conservative: they produce significantly fewer False Alarms ($27.46\%$ and $31.93\%$), but exhibit higher Miss rates ($44.12\%$ and $40.97\%$) on low-energy 8 kHz telephony speech.
2. **Speaker Confusion:**
   - On the 3-speaker telephony recording `call_input01`, **Model 02** achieved the lowest confusion ($3.02\%$), followed by **Model 04** ($3.96\%$), whereas **Model 01** exhibited $25.39\%$ confusion between overlapping speakers.
3. **Clean Presentation Audio:**
   - On `liveTranscription_input02` (VoxConverse), all three models demonstrated production-grade performance ($3.67\%$ to $4.99\%$ DER), with NeMo Sortformer Offline delivering near-flawless zero-confusion segmentation.

## 8. Benchmark Limitations & Observations

1. **Dataset Size:** A 3-recording benchmark provides valuable directional insight into model behaviors across different acoustic domains (broadcast presentation vs. 8 kHz telephony), but sample sizes are insufficient for statistical generalization across entire speaker populations.
2. **Telephony vs. Studio Audio:** Models trained primarily on 16 kHz wideband speech (e.g. Sortformer) require careful acoustic handling when processing 8 kHz narrowband NIST U-Law telephony recordings.
3. **Reference Integrity:** Pre-packaged ground-truth annotations must always be audited for time-offset drifts from master recordings, as demonstrated by the 400s offset discovery on `call_input02`.
